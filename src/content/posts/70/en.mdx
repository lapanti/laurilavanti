Public transport fares will rise again. HSL, the Helsinki region transport authority, plans to raise fares by about three percent a year in its preliminary plan for 2027–2029. By the plan's end, prices would be nearly ten percent higher than today. That does not serve Kirkkonummi residents.

## Why does a flat percentage hit Kirkkonummi hardest?

When fares rise by the same percentage in every zone, those living furthest away pay most. In Kirkkonummi that means commuters using public transport. Commuting and other regular trips to Helsinki require an ABCD ticket, and under HSL's current fare table a 30-day season ticket costs an adult 121.80 euros. The same percentage costs a Kirkkonummi resident more in euros than someone travelling from Espoo to Helsinki, who can use a cheaper two- or three-zone ticket.

## Why did we oppose the increase in the municipality's statement?

Member municipalities are submitting their statements on the plan. Kirkkonummi's Urban Development Committee reviewed the statement in August, and the Municipal Board on the last day of the month. We Greens opposed the fare increases, because raising ticket prices is not the right way to balance HSL's finances. We raised the same concern in [our earlier piece on public transport pricing](/en/blog/41/public-transport-cannot-keep-getting-more-expensive/), and the underlying situation has not changed since.

## What does the increase do to zoning around the stations?

Kirkkonummi zones station areas and rail corridors so that growth settles where the train runs. The same thinking was behind [my vote for the Western Rail Link](/en/blog/46/i-voted-for-the-western-rail-link/) and our assessment of [what the decision revealed about the municipality's strategic development](/en/blog/50/the-west-railway-decision-revealed-problems-in-kirkkonummis-long-term-strategic-development/). That investment only pays off if the train remains a reasonable alternative to the car. Every increase narrows the gap on which the zoning and [the goal of a vibrant centre](/en/blog/35/a-growing-municipality-needs-a-vibrant-centre/) depend.

## What would fix HSL's funding model?

HSL's finances are strained, but large fare increases will not fix that. Operating costs keep rising, and larger municipal contributions would burden municipalities heavily in the current economic situation. The choice is not between raising prices and doing nothing. The consultation round is when municipalities can demand that the funding model be corrected. Ending the infrastructure compensation agreement, under which other HSL municipalities pay for big cities' infrastructure projects, would improve HSL's finances considerably and spread costs more fairly.

## Who will travel by public transport ten years from now?

A rail connection is basic infrastructure, like a road or a water main. As public transport costs shift onto the user year by year, the service becomes one only the well-off can afford. In a municipality the size of Kirkkonummi, the change shows within a single planning period. For many, the car is already the only realistic option, and an expensive ticket makes it sensible too. Yet the municipality's strategy calls for more public transport use. That is the contradiction.

A single fare increase is small. Together, these increases decide whether Kirkkonummi residents travel by public transport or by car ten years from now. More of my writing on [transport and public transport](/en/category/transportation/) is collected on the topic page.
